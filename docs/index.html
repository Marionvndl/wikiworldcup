How are the levels defined?

The difficulty of a game depends on its start and target articles. We use two main criteria, both known before anyone plays.

1. Shortest path

Wikipedia articles are connected by links, which form a network. The shortest path between two articles is the smallest number of clicks needed to go from one to the other, if the player always chose the best link. The longer it is, the more chances the player has to take a wrong turn.

Links only go one way, so the distance from A to B can differ from the distance from B to A.

2. PageRank of the target

PageRank measures how important an article is in the network. Imagine a player who clicks on random links forever: the PageRank of an article is the share of time this player spends on it. An article is important if many articles link to it, and even more so if those articles are important themselves. A target with a high PageRank can be reached from many directions, so it is easier to find.

PR(A) = 1 − dN + d · ΣB → A PR(B)L(B)
  • PR(A): PageRank of article A
  • B → A: every article B that has a link to A
  • L(B): number of links leaving article B, so B shares its importance among all the pages it links to
  • N: number of articles (4,604 in Wikispeedia)
  • d = 0.85: probability that the random player follows a link instead of jumping to a random article

From criteria to levels: our model

We trained a model (a logistic regression) on more than 42,000 past games. From the information available before play, it predicts the chance that a player wins a game. The two criteria above carry most of the information. The model also uses the PageRank and number of links of the start article, and how close the two topics are.

  1. Each game gets a predicted chance of winning.
  2. Its difficulty score is 1 − predicted chance of winning.
  3. Games are sorted by score and split into five groups of the same size: level 1 holds the 20% easiest games, level 5 the 20% hardest.
